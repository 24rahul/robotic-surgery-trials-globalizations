Title: Globalization of Robotic Surgery Clinical Trials: A 25-Year Bibliometric Analysis

Background: Robotic surgery has diffused across surgical disciplines over the past 25 years, yet the extent and geography of clinical trial activity are not well described.

Objective: To quantify globalization, patterns, and trends in robotic surgery clinical trials (PublicationType: Randomized Controlled Trial or Clinical Trial) between 2001–2025.

Methods: PubMed baseline and updates were processed to identify surgery-related articles, from which robotic surgery records were filtered using MeSH terms and title/abstract keywords. True clinical trials were retained when PublicationType included “Randomized Controlled Trial” or contained “Clinical Trial.” Outcomes included: annual trial counts; number of active countries per year; multi-country collaboration share; average countries per trial; Herfindahl–Hirschman Index (HHI) of country concentration; and regional shares (Europe, Asia, North America, Oceania, Latin America, Middle East, Africa). Country attribution used author affiliations when available or journal country as fallback.

Results: Annual clinical trials increased from 26 in 2001 to 292 in 2025. Countries active per year expanded from 9–12 (early) to 24–27 (recent). Multi-country collaboration rose from 3.8% (2001) to 26.4% (2025), with average countries per trial increasing from ~1.00 to ~1.32. Geographic concentration declined (HHI ~0.18–0.24 early to ~0.09–0.12 recent). Regional shares shifted: Asia 0.269→0.527; Europe 0.385→0.555; North America 0.346→0.195. Overall leaders (2001–2025) were the United States (676), China (322), United Kingdom (293), Germany (203), and South Korea (187). In the last 5 years, China (238) led, followed by the United States (201), United Kingdom (141), Germany (88), and Italy (71). Comparator language was common (laparoscopic n=738; open n=338), indicating ongoing head-to-head evaluation against conventional approaches.

Conclusions: Robotic surgery clinical trials have expanded and globalized substantially over the past 25 years, with increased multi-country collaboration and decreasing geographic concentration. Asia and Europe now contribute the largest regional shares, while the United States remains a leading contributor and China has emerged rapidly in the last 5 years. These trends reflect a broadening global evidence base for robotic surgery across surgical specialties.
